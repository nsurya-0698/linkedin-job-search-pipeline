# Job-Linked Interview Preparation

Create an interview packet for each high-priority role or active interview. Tie every topic and story to the current JD and candidate evidence.

## Packet Contents

Include:

- company, team, product, and role summary with source dates;
- the five to ten most important responsibilities/requirements;
- likely coding, backend, cloud, platform, distributed-systems, AI/GenAI, security, reliability, or data topics supported by the JD;
- likely system-design scope and operational constraints;
- behavioral and ownership themes;
- three to six resume stories with exact source evidence;
- candidate weak points, unknowns, and honest bridging language;
- questions for recruiter, hiring manager, engineers, and final-round interviewers;
- stage, interview date, interviewer names/roles when supplied, and follow-up tasks.

Do not turn a preferred qualification into a guaranteed interview topic. Label each item `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, or `LOW` confidence and explain the signal.

## Resume Story Cards

For each likely story, record:

- situation and scope;
- the candidate's specific responsibility;
- actions the candidate can substantiate;
- result using only verified metrics;
- technical tradeoffs;
- failure, ambiguity, or learning when relevant;
- JD competency addressed;
- source resume/profile entry.

If ownership or metrics are unclear, ask the candidate rather than writing a polished but unsupported STAR story.

## Stage-Based Focus

- **Recruiter screen:** motivation, role fit, concise career narrative, location/work model, compensation boundaries, and candidate-confirmed authorization answers.
- **Hiring-manager screen:** scope, ownership, collaboration, architecture decisions, delivery, and role-specific outcomes.
- **Technical:** language/runtime depth, APIs, data, debugging, testing, algorithms, and domain technologies from the JD.
- **System design:** requirements, scale, data model, interfaces, availability, security, observability, deployment, and tradeoffs.
- **Final/behavioral:** leadership, conflict, ambiguity, failure, cross-functional influence, and long-term fit.

Never answer legally significant questions for the user. Prepare neutral prompts and request the candidate's exact wording.

## After Each Interview

Record stage, date, topics asked, evidence used, surprises, weak answers, interviewer signals, thank-you draft, next step, and outcome. Convert repeated gaps into preparation priorities, not invented resume skills.
