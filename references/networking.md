# Relationship-Focused LinkedIn Networking

Optimize for relevant professional relationships, not connection count. Networking research and drafting are allowed in preparation mode; sending is execution mode.

## Contact Priority

Use this strict hierarchy for every contact search, shortlist, and outreach batch:

1. **Recruiters:** corporate, technical, agency, staffing, talent-acquisition, and sourcing professionals with credible job, company, role-family, or geographic relevance.
2. **Hiring managers:** confirmed or strongly evidenced managers for the relevant role, team, or job family.
3. **Other relevant professionals:** directors, technical leaders, staff/principal/senior engineers, and employees with genuine technical or company overlap.

Fill the requested batch from recruiters first. Move to hiring managers only when there are not enough eligible recruiters, and move to other professionals only when the first two tiers are insufficient. A lower-tier contact must not displace an eligible higher-tier contact. When presenting a lower-tier contact, state why the higher tiers were insufficient.

Relevance is still mandatory. Do not select an unrelated recruiter merely to satisfy the hierarchy or inflate connection volume.

Do not target people solely because they are senior or highly visible.

## Contact Score

Score each dimension from 0 to 5 and preserve the evidence:

- role relevance;
- company and target-team relevance;
- hiring/recruiting relevance;
- technical relevance;
- personalization evidence;
- plausible mutual professional value.

Apply the strict tier hierarchy before comparing scores. Within the same tier, weight target-team and hiring relevance twice. Rank contacts within a specific job/company context; a global “best contacts” list is less useful.

Store name, title, company, verified profile URL, contact category, why relevant, related job, factual personalization point, connection status, last contact, next follow-up, response status, and source date.

## Personalization Rules

Use only visible, attributable professional facts such as the person's current role, a public technical post, a public talk, an open-source contribution, or direct connection to the advertised team. Keep the relevance explanation in the record.

Never fabricate or imply:

- shared history, employer, school, event, or mutual connection;
- a prior conversation or relationship;
- knowledge of private work;
- enthusiasm for content that was not inspected;
- a referral expectation.

Avoid collecting or storing unrelated personal information.

## Draft Types

Support concise drafts for connection requests, recruiters, hiring managers, post-connection notes, application follow-ups, informational conversations, and thank-you messages.

A good draft normally contains:

1. a truthful reason for contacting this person;
2. one specific shared technical/job context;
3. a small, non-pushy request or observation;
4. a professional close.

Do not mass-produce near-identical messages. If no honest personalization point exists, recommend following the person or engaging with relevant public work before requesting time.

## Approval and Follow-Up

Before sending, show the exact recipient, message, linked job/company, and any personal data being transmitted. Obtain explicit approval for that message. Respect platform limits and do not bypass CAPTCHAs or anti-automation controls.

Set follow-ups based on real context rather than aggressive cadence. One polite follow-up may be appropriate after a reasonable interval; stop after a decline, no-contact request, platform warning, or clear lack of relevance. Record responses and conversations so repeated outreach is avoided.
